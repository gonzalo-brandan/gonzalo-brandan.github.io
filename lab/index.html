[ Where I'm going ]

Infrastructure people can rely on

Every team depends on things most people never see: the network, the accounts, the servers and the laptops on the desks. I want to be one of the people who keep all of that running, and who leave it a little better and better documented than they found it.

I'm starting broad on purpose. Support, systems and networks each teach you how the pieces fit together. Further out, I'm curious about large-scale infrastructure, including the compute Europe is building for AI.

[ What I enjoy · How I work ]

Hands-on, and patient with problems and people

I'd rather spend my day fixing and building than in meetings: tracing a fault, talking to the person who is stuck, and working out how the hardware, the systems and the network fit together. I have a soft spot for a well-dressed rack, which is partly why there's one on this page. Most of what I know comes from reading and from trying things out in my homelab, and I enjoy the problems that take a while to figure out.

Before IT, I ran guest relations at a 1,300-guest hostel in Berlin. I handled 60 to 80 escalated cases a week in German, English and Spanish, and answered most of them within four hours. That's where I learned to stay calm with someone who is stuck, find the real cause and write the fix down for the next person.

[ What I care about ]

Technology for people

I care about open source, privacy, an open and inclusive society, and technology that genuinely improves people's lives. I try to bring those values into the way I work: respect people's choices, share knowledge, and build things that are useful.

Technology is only as fair as the people who build it.
